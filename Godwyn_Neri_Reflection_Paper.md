### My Growth from 1st Year to 4th Year (1st Semester)

#### 1. Academic Growth
* **1st Year:** I focused on foundational subjects like *Computer Programming* and *Understanding the Self*. I learned basic programming logic, but my understanding was limited to writing small programs that printed text to a console.
* **2nd Year:** My subjects became more practical and challenging, including *Data Communications*, *Network Technology*, and *Object-Oriented Programming*. I began to understand how networks operate, how computers talk to each other, and how software interacts with databases.
* **3rd Year:** I learned about *IT Service Management (ITSM)*, system architecture, and advanced web development. I stopped looking at code as isolated files and started viewing it as complete systems that serve business and user needs.
* **4th Year (1st Semester):** In *IT Capstone Project 2*, I led the development of **Lumina LMS**, a library management and student attendance system for STI College Alabang. Using Next.js, TypeScript, Supabase, and Microsoft Azure, I handled real requirements like user authentication, QR/barcode attendance scanning, and database security. Academically, I went from just memorizing lessons to building functional, production-ready systems.

#### 2. Personal Growth
Personally, my biggest challenge was dealing with self-doubt and impostor syndrome. In the beginning, when I encountered hard coding bugs or system errors, I felt overwhelmed and wondered if I was cut out for IT.

Over time, I learned patience and emotional control. Fixing computers for clients as a freelance technician and spending long hours debugging code taught me to stay calm. Instead of stressing out, I learned to break problems down step-by-step. I also learned time management: balancing capstone work, school subjects, club duties, and freelance work required me to set priorities and stay disciplined.

#### 3. Professional Growth
My professional growth came from experiences both inside and outside the classroom:
* **Coder's Club Auditor:** Serving as the Auditor for the Coder's Club during the first semester of my third year taught me responsibility and transparency. I managed organization records and helped organize workshops where I guided other students in web development and Git.
* **Work Immersion:** Completing 180 hours of immersion at *Barato Family Restaurant* taught me how a workplace functions daily. I designed marketing materials, assisted with customer service, and learned workplace cooperation.
* **Freelance Computer Technician:** Repairing laptops and desktops for clients taught me customer communication, accountability, and the importance of delivering quality work on time.

Through these experiences, I practiced STI's 4Cs: **Character** (honesty in my work), **Critical Thinking** (troubleshooting problems logically), **Communication** (explaining technical ideas clearly), and **Change-Adeptness** (learning new tools quickly).
